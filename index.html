01 Work

Loop · study plans onto Google Calendar

Give Loop a career goal and your free hours and it puts a study plan on your Google Calendar. Language-model nodes draft the plan; ordinary code validates and schedules it, and nothing lands on the calendar until you approve. Product prompts are pinned by hash, so editing one fails the build until it is re-pinned. Each push to main re-grades saved model outputs against fixed floors for schema validity and citation coverage. The retrieval test behind the plan's citations still runs by hand.

Foothold · which courses transfer

A California community college student enters their courses and a target UC or CSU and learns which will transfer. The verdict is deterministic over 352,024 rows of official ASSIST transfer agreements, and findings cite the agreement behind them. One Claude node drafts the appeal letter for at-risk courses, and every course code in it is checked against the findings before the letter reaches the student. I built it solo in three weeks for Stellic Pathfinders 2026, where it was featured (top 20% of 169 submissions). The agreements are 2025-26, with no refresh yet.

Crash Anticipation · dashcam collision warnings

A model that watches dashcam video and warns before a crash. I distilled a VideoMAE teacher into a 2.95M-parameter MobileNetV2-GRU that streams at 2.45 ms per frame (408 fps, RTX 5070 Ti). On average it warns 3.05 seconds before impact, with 3 false alarms in 301 no-crash videos. My first benchmark held only crashes, so a model that always predicted a crash scored perfectly, and I rebuilt it with real no-crash videos.

Arrhythmia Classifier · MIT-BIH leakage study

A course project with two classmates: we showed that a beat-wise train/test split on MIT-BIH leaks patient identity. In one run, the same 64,966-parameter CNN scores 98.36% beat-wise and 83.88% on a random patient-wise split. My search over 50,000 sampled patient assignments found a patient-wise split that keeps all six beat classes in the test set, but nothing has been trained on it yet. Many published MIT-BIH results use a beat-wise split. I'd rather report the 83.88.
